How AbegEppMe Is Transforming Access to Trusted Service Providers in Nigeria

July 2026 8 min read DialX Editorial
Home› Blog› AbegEppMe

Picture this: your kitchen tap starts leaking on a Saturday morning. You call two plumbers who don't show up. A third quotes you triple the going rate, then collects a deposit and stops picking up. By Monday, your floor is waterlogged and you are out of both money and patience.

If you have lived in Nigeria for any length of time, that story doesn't read like a worst-case scenario — it reads like a regular experience. Finding a reliable, skilled service provider has long been one of those low-grade, everyday frustrations that Nigerians have simply learned to manage. You ask a neighbour. You post in a WhatsApp group. You take a chance and hope for the best.

A Nigerian startup called AbegEppMe thinks there is a better way.

What Is AbegEppMe?

AbegEppMe is a Nigerian service marketplace that connects customers with verified artisans, handymen, skilled professionals, and service providers through a structured digital platform. The name — a phrase drawn straight from Nigerian Pidgin — tells you exactly who the product is for and what it is trying to do: help people get the help they need. Easily. Reliably. Without the usual drama.

Customers can search for professionals, book services, make secure payments, and manage jobs from one place. Service providers, on the other hand, gain access to more customers and business opportunities than they could ever reach through word-of-mouth alone.

The platform covers a wide range of service categories:

  • Home maintenance and repairs
  • Cleaning services
  • Event services
  • Logistics
  • Health
  • Education
  • Business services
  • Skilled trades
  • Professional services
  • Technology services

Whether you need a plumber, an electrician, an event decorator, a private tutor, or a graphic designer, AbegEppMe is designed to be the first place you check — instead of the five WhatsApp groups and three Facebook posts that currently define the search process for most people.

The Problem Is Bigger Than One Leaky Tap

To understand why AbegEppMe matters, it helps to understand just how broken the current system is — for both customers and service providers.

The customer's struggle

Finding a reliable artisan or professional in Nigeria is not just inconvenient — it carries genuine risk. Verification is nearly impossible. When you find someone through a referral or social media search, you're essentially taking a gamble. There's no formal record of their work, no way to check what previous clients experienced, and no accountability structure if things go badly.

Fraud is real and common. Taking a deposit and disappearing is a pattern so widespread that many Nigerians now expect it as a possibility in every service transaction. Without a platform that holds payments and enforces accountability, customers bear all the risk of every job.

Quality is inconsistent. Even when a service provider shows up and completes a job, quality can vary dramatically from one person to the next. Without standardised expectations or a review system, there is no way to distinguish the skilled from the careless before the money changes hands.

Discovery is limited to your network. The most talented carpenter in your area might work three streets away — but if they are not connected to someone you know, they might as well not exist. Most service bookings in Nigeria still happen through personal referrals, which means your options are only as wide as your immediate social circle.

The service provider's invisible wall

For artisans and skilled professionals, the other side of this problem is equally limiting. Nigeria has enormous numbers of genuinely talented people — plumbers, electricians, caterers, designers, technicians — who are practically invisible outside their immediate neighbourhoods. They rely on slow-moving word-of-mouth, have no formal channel to showcase their work, and no efficient way to manage bookings, payments, or client relationships.

The result is a paradox: customers can't find the professionals they need, and professionals can't reach the customers who would hire them. AbegEppMe is trying to break that deadlock from both ends.

"The best plumber in your city might be three streets away. But if they're not in your circle, you'll never know they exist."

How AbegEppMe Solves It

AbegEppMe's approach centres on what it calls trust, accountability, quality service, and convenience — all within a single, structured digital experience. It is the kind of platform that replaces the gamble with a process.

For customers

Customers can browse verified professional profiles, read reviews from past clients, book services directly through the platform, and make secure payments without the anxiety of handing money to someone who might disappear. Everything — discovery, booking, payment, and job management — happens in one place, with the platform holding both parties accountable throughout the process.

For service providers

Service providers get a professional digital profile that works like a constantly working advertisement — visible to potential customers whether the provider is asleep, on a job, or unavailable to take a call. Beyond visibility, the platform gives them tools to manage their business: bookings, job tracking, and payments handled without the friction that often plagues informal service arrangements.

Critically, AbegEppMe also brings the kind of accountability that builds long-term trust. Reviews, verified profiles, and a structured payment system all push service providers toward higher standards — which is good for customers and, ultimately, good for the providers themselves.

Why This Goes Beyond Convenience

It is easy to look at a service marketplace and see it as a useful app. But the impact of what AbegEppMe is building runs deeper than making it easier to find a plumber.

It empowers artisans and skilled workers. Nigeria's artisan community is large, talented, and largely invisible on the formal internet. A skilled carpenter who has built beautiful furniture for twenty years might have almost no digital presence and no way to grow beyond the small circle of people who already know him. AbegEppMe gives those people a stage — and, more importantly, a market.

It supports small business growth. For many service providers, the limiting factor isn't skill — it's distribution. They can do the work; they simply can't reach enough people who need it. Digital visibility changes that equation, and at scale, it creates real economic movement.

It builds consumer confidence. When customers trust the process — when they know they can leave reviews, dispute problems, and rely on payment protection — they're more willing to spend and try services they'd otherwise avoid. That increased confidence is good for the entire service economy.

It contributes to Nigeria's digital economy. Every verified booking on AbegEppMe is a transaction that happens within an accountable, structured system rather than informally. Over time, that matters for data, business formalisation, and the kind of economic infrastructure that makes markets work better for everyone.

The Bigger Picture: Technology Meets Trust in Africa

Platforms like AbegEppMe aren't just about one company's growth — they represent a meaningful shift in how Africa organises and delivers everyday services.

Across the continent, the informal economy is vast. That informality has always carried its own kind of creativity and resilience. But it has also meant millions of skilled people unable to reach their full market, and millions of customers unable to access the quality of service they're willing to pay for — simply because the infrastructure for trust didn't exist.

Technology is changing that. Digital service marketplaces are bringing structure, transparency, and scale to sectors that have long operated on handshakes and hope. In doing so, they are creating more opportunities for entrepreneurship, more efficient markets, and more trust between strangers who need each other.

AbegEppMe is a Nigerian-built answer to a Nigerian problem — with the potential to scale as the country's digital infrastructure continues to deepen. It is the kind of company that quietly makes life a little less stressful for ordinary people, while simultaneously creating real economic opportunity for the skilled professionals who make those services possible.
